## Scene 001

[natural pace] [warm] [welcoming]

السلام عليكم. Welcome back to ROOTS. We now begin the F domain: Focusing on Learner Behaviour. Its first principle is Individualization. At NAMA Foundation, we use this principle to keep each learner visible within the complete learning experience. [short pause] By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain Individualization and identify teacher decisions that respond to learner differences while maintaining a shared outcome. Think about a class you know well. What differences between learners influence how they enter, experience, and respond to the same lesson?

## Scene 002

[natural pace] [clear] [explanatory]

In ROOTS, Individualization means making the learning experience unique for every learner whenever possible. It includes building a direct relationship with each individual during the learning event. [short pause] This does not mean that every learner must receive a completely separate course. It means that the teacher remains conscious of individuals rather than seeing only one group. The learner’s responses, needs, knowledge, feelings, abilities, and experience become information that can guide how the teacher designs, explains, questions, supports, and follows learning.

## Scene 003

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [teacher-focused]

The F domain focuses on the learner’s behaviour throughout the learning journey and on how the teacher engages with each individual. ROOTS asks us to consider the learner’s curve of learning, previous knowledge, feelings, and experience. [short pause] These elements affect how a learner receives a question, approaches an activity, understands an explanation, or responds to feedback. Individualization begins when the teacher notices that learners may share one outcome while reaching it with different starting points, responses, and support needs. Awareness comes before any adjustment can be meaningful.

## Scene 004

[natural pace] [reassuring] [conversational]

Individualization can be difficult to apply with every learner at every moment. In ROOTS, we recognize this challenge and ask teachers to remain aware and make a conscious effort whenever possible. [short pause] The goal is not perfection or constant one-to-one teaching. The goal is deliberate attention. A teacher can notice patterns without losing sight of individuals, create moments to hear learner thinking, and use available opportunities to understand how different learners are progressing. Small, informed decisions can make the experience feel more responsive without removing its shared direction.

## Scene 005

[natural pace] [warm] [practical]

A direct relationship helps the teacher understand the learner more clearly. This relationship can develop through a question, a brief conversation about the learner’s work, careful listening, or attention during practice. In ROOTS, even a break can become an opportunity to check in and strengthen Individualization. [short pause] What matters is purposeful contact. The teacher is not collecting personal detail without reason. The teacher is learning what may support participation and progress. Respectful attention can reveal previous understanding, uncertainty, confidence, interest, or a need for further guidance.

## Scene 006

[natural pace] [focused] [teacher-focused]

Evidence makes Individualization practical. A learner’s answer, question, written response, demonstration, or behaviour during an activity can show how that learner is experiencing the lesson. The teacher can use different questions, observe work, or hold a short conversation to understand what needs monitoring. [short pause] This evidence should connect with the intended outcome. Individualization is not based only on assumption or preference. It becomes a response to what the teacher can notice and understand about learning, allowing support to be more precise and the next decision to be more purposeful.

## Scene 007

[natural pace] [engaged] [clear]

Once the teacher notices a difference, several decisions may follow. One learner may need clarification, another may be ready for a deeper question, and another may need an additional opportunity to practise or explain. The teacher may adjust the question, the amount of support, the pace of a step, or the way evidence is gathered. [short pause] These are not automatic actions. The appropriate response depends on the learner, the outcome, and the evidence available. Individualization connects attention with decision: notice the learner, interpret the response carefully, and choose support that helps learning move forward.

## Scene 008

[natural pace] [steady] [explanatory]

A shared learning outcome and an individualized experience can exist together. The outcome identifies the significant learning that learners should demonstrate. Individualization considers how different learners may need to engage, practise, respond, or receive support as they work toward it. [short pause] The teacher does not lower the outcome simply because learners begin from different points. Instead, the teacher examines the route and the evidence. This distinction keeps the lesson learner-centred without losing clarity. The destination remains visible while teaching decisions respond to how learners are progressing toward it.

## Scene 009

[natural pace] [reflective] [encouraging]

Individualization also asks the teacher to remain open to what learners already bring. Previous knowledge and experience may support new learning, while feelings and confidence may influence participation. A learner’s silence, quick answer, hesitation, or willingness to try should not be interpreted without attention to context. [short pause] ROOTS encourages teachers to probe, observe, and build understanding rather than act on a single impression. The purpose is to treat learner behaviour as information to explore, so that the teacher’s response is guided by evidence and connected with learning.

## Scene 010

[natural pace] [practical] [conversational]

During design, ask where the lesson creates opportunities to notice individuals. Is there a question that reveals previous knowledge? Is there practice that makes different responses visible? Is there a moment for learners to explain their thinking? During facilitation, ask whose learning is visible and whose is still unclear. [short pause] These questions connect Individualization with Learner eXperience Design and Learner eXperience Facilitation. The teacher plans opportunities for evidence and then uses those opportunities to respond. Design prepares the possibility; facilitation turns attention into action.

## Scene 011

[natural pace] [inviting] [reflective]

Choose one lesson and identify a shared outcome. Then select three learners or three different response patterns you have observed. For each one, note the evidence available: previous knowledge, a question, a demonstration, participation, difficulty, or progress. [short pause] Ask what small adjustment could help each learner move toward the same outcome. You may change a question, add practice, offer clarification, invite an explanation, or observe further before deciding. This reflection evaluates the principle through a real teaching decision without requiring you to redesign the entire lesson at once.

## Scene 012

[slow pace] [warm] [confident]

Individualization keeps the individual learner visible. It asks teachers to notice previous knowledge, feelings, experience, behaviour, and the learner’s developing curve of learning. It does not require a separate lesson for every person, but it does require conscious attention and purposeful response whenever possible. [short pause] In ROOTS, the teacher connects evidence with adjustments while keeping the shared outcome clear. In the next lesson, we will study Probing and Assessing, the second F principle, and examine how teachers create and use opportunities to understand learning and guide the next decision.
