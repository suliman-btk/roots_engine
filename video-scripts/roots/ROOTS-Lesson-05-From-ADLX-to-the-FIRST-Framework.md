## Scene 001

[natural pace] [warm] [welcoming]

السلام عليكم. Welcome back to ROOTS. We have established four foundations: the learner-centred shift, the teacher’s purposeful role, Active Deep Learner eXperience, and the alignment of outcomes with assessment. At NAMA Foundation, we now bring those foundations into the FIRST Framework. [short pause] By the end of this lesson, you should be able to name its five domains, recognize the fifteen principles, and explain how the domains work together to support ADLX. Think of FIRST as a connected view of the learner experience, not as five separate lists or a collection of unrelated teaching techniques.

## Scene 002

[natural pace] [clear] [confident]

The ROOTS Programme Manual explains that Active Deep Learner eXperience is the foundation of FIRST. The framework contains five primary domains and fifteen principles, with three principles in each domain. ADLX is formed when the principles are integrated into a cohesive whole. [short pause] The framework brings attention to the learner, interaction, activity, sequence, and the movement of learning into performance. Each domain helps us examine part of the experience, while the complete framework helps us see how those parts connect during design and facilitation.

## Scene 003

[natural pace] [explanatory] [steady]

The five domains function as layers. Each adds an important perspective and strengthens the complete learner experience. We first focus on the individual learner. We then consider interaction and group dynamics. We examine readiness, purposeful activity, and active review. We connect activities through structure and sequence. Finally, we support the movement from learning into practice and performance. [short pause] The layers are connected rather than ranked. A teacher uses them to notice relationships across the whole experience and to avoid treating one engaging activity as the complete design.

## Scene 004

[natural pace] [focused] [teacher-focused]

F stands for Focusing on Learner Behaviour. This domain asks the teacher to consider each learner’s prior knowledge, feelings, experience, learning curve, and responses throughout the learning journey. Its three principles are Individualization, Probing and Assessing, and Trusting the Learner. [short pause] Together, they guide us to notice learner differences, create and use evidence for teaching decisions, and recognize learners as active participants with knowledge, experience, ability, and responsibility. F keeps the individual learner visible even when teaching takes place within a larger class.

## Scene 005

[natural pace] [engaged] [clear]

I stands for Interacting within Positive Group Dynamics. While F looks closely at each learner, I considers interaction between learners and the experience created by the group. Its three principles are Learning as a Social Event, Creating a Positive Spirit, and Supporting Motivation and Attention. [short pause] These principles remind us that interaction, emotion, motivation, focus, and relationships influence participation and learning. The teacher considers how the social environment supports the outcome, rather than assuming that placing learners together automatically creates a positive or productive experience.

## Scene 006

[natural pace] [practical] [explanatory]

R stands for Reviewing Activities within RAR. RAR directs attention to the full experience of an activity: readiness before it begins, facilitation while learners participate, and active review afterward. Its three principles are Increasing Learner Readiness, Facilitating Learner Activity, and Reviewing Learning Actively. [short pause] The activity itself is not the whole learning process. Learners need preparation to enter it, support to participate meaningfully, and review that helps them use the experience for learning and development. In ROOTS, active review is where experience is examined and meaning becomes visible.

## Scene 007

[natural pace] [clear] [steady]

S stands for Sequencing Activities. This domain considers the complete flow of the learning experience from beginning to end and how its parts connect. Its three principles are Structuring and Sequencing Learning, Repetition without Boredom, and Linking and Summarizing. [short pause] Structure helps ideas and activities make sense together. Purposeful variation and repetition support the development of knowledge and skill without reducing learning to redundant drill. Linking and summarizing help learners monitor the learning path, see connections, and consolidate what the experience is building.

## Scene 008

[natural pace] [confident] [teacher-focused]

T stands for Transforming Learning into Performance. This domain asks how learning begins to move into action and how practice and transfer are supported. Its three principles are Reflection on Reality, Practising and Experiencing, and Continuity and Follow-Up. [short pause] Learners connect the topic with their own reality, receive opportunities to practise and experience what they are learning, and continue the learning beyond the immediate event. T keeps performance in view and reminds the teacher that understanding gains strength when learners connect it with reality, action, feedback, and continued application.

## Scene 009

[natural pace] [analytical] [engaged]

Now notice how the domains connect. F helps us understand and respond to the learner. I considers the interaction and conditions surrounding participation. R shapes readiness, activity, and review. S organizes the complete flow and connects its parts. T supports the movement of learning into practice and performance. [short pause] Removing one layer can weaken the whole experience. Activity without readiness or review may lose meaning. Sequence without attention to learner response may become rigid. Practice without connection or feedback may repeat current performance. FIRST helps us examine these relationships together.

## Scene 010

[natural pace] [practical] [conversational]

For teachers, the framework supports both design and facilitation. During design, we can use the domains to examine the intended outcome, learner differences, interaction, activity, sequence, practice, and follow-up. During facilitation, we can observe learner behaviour, support participation, guide activity, help learners review, make connections visible, and respond to evidence. [short pause] FIRST is not a checklist to display without application. Its value comes from using the principles to make connected decisions that strengthen the Active Deep Learner eXperience in a particular learning context.

## Scene 011

[natural pace] [inviting] [reflective]

Choose one lesson or learning experience you know well and scan it through the five domains. Where do you consider individual learners? How does interaction support learning? What prepares learners for activity, and how is the activity reviewed? How are ideas and experiences sequenced and linked? What helps learning move toward reality, practice, and continued action? [short pause] You are not trying to apply all fifteen principles at once. This first review helps you notice which layers are already visible and which relationships deserve closer attention as we study each domain in depth.

## Scene 012

[slow pace] [warm] [confident]

FIRST contains five connected domains: Focusing on Learner Behaviour, Interacting within Positive Group Dynamics, Reviewing Activities within RAR, Sequencing Activities, and Transforming Learning into Performance. Each domain contains three principles, creating fifteen principles that work together to support ADLX. [short pause] In ROOTS, we will study every principle while keeping the complete learner experience in view. In the next lesson, we begin with the F domain and its first principle, Individualization, examining how awareness of learner differences can guide design and facilitation decisions.
