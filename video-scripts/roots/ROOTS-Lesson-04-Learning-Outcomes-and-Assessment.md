## Scene 001

[natural pace] [warm] [welcoming]

السلام عليكم. Welcome back to ROOTS. We have explored learner-centred teaching and Active Deep Learner eXperience. At NAMA Foundation, we now connect that experience with a clear direction and evidence that shows where learning stands. This is the relationship between learning outcomes and assessment. [short pause] By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain how an outcome guides content, activity, and teacher decisions, and identify assessment evidence that supports learning. Begin with one question: when your lesson ends, what should learners be able to demonstrate that they could not demonstrate before?

## Scene 002

[natural pace] [clear] [explanatory]

The ROOTS knowledge material describes learning outcomes as statements of significant and essential learning that learners have achieved and can reliably demonstrate at the end of a course or programme. An outcome identifies what the learner will know, be able to do, or be willing to demonstrate. [short pause] This wording matters because it places the result with the learner. An outcome is not simply a topic heading or a list of what the teacher intends to explain. It describes the change in knowledge, skill, attitude, or performance that the learning experience is intended to support.

## Scene 003

[natural pace] [reflective] [teacher-focused]

Consider the difference between these two planning questions: “What content will I cover?” and “What should learners demonstrate?” The first question directs attention toward the teacher’s delivery. The second directs attention toward learning. Both content and explanation still matter, but they are selected in service of the outcome. [short pause] In ROOTS, we use outcomes to connect the learner-centred shift with practical planning. Once the expected learning is clear, the teacher can make more deliberate decisions about content, questions, activity, practice, review, and the evidence to observe.

## Scene 004

[natural pace] [steady] [clear]

The ROOTS Programme Manual groups learning outcomes into three domains: knowledge, skill, and attitude. Knowledge includes ideas, facts, and models that a learner understands. Skill concerns an ability the learner can perform. Attitude concerns inclination or willingness; being capable of an action does not necessarily mean a person chooses to carry it out. [short pause] These domains help a teacher clarify the kind of learning expected. They also remind us that one form of evidence may not be suitable for every outcome. The evidence should match the knowledge, skill, or attitude being examined.

## Scene 005

[natural pace] [practical] [focused]

A useful outcome makes the expected learning visible enough to guide decisions. If an outcome concerns knowledge, learners may need to explain, distinguish, connect, or apply an idea. If it concerns skill, they need an opportunity to perform or demonstrate. If it concerns attitude, evidence must relate to the intended willingness or tendency rather than knowledge alone. [short pause] ROOTS asks us to keep the learner’s result at the centre. The wording of an outcome should help the teacher recognize what evidence to seek and help learners understand the direction of their effort.

## Scene 006

[natural pace] [analytical] [teacher-focused]

Once the outcome is clear, we can examine alignment. The content should provide the knowledge and understanding needed for the outcome. The activity should give learners an appropriate way to engage with that learning. Practice should prepare them to demonstrate it. Assessment should then gather evidence that matches what the outcome asks. [short pause] If learners are expected to demonstrate a skill but only recall information, the evidence is incomplete. If an activity is engaging but unrelated to the outcome, it may not support the intended learning. Alignment keeps every part moving in the same direction.

## Scene 007

[natural pace] [warm] [explanatory]

Assessment is not something that belongs only at the end. The ROOTS Programme Manual describes it as a dynamic and integral part of the learning process that supports understanding, improvement, motivation, accountability, and continuous development. [short pause] During teaching, assessment helps us see what learners understand, what they can do, and where they may need further support. It gives the teacher information for the next decision and gives learners information about their progress. In this sense, assessment becomes part of learning rather than an interruption added after learning is complete.

## Scene 008

[natural pace] [emphasis] [clear]

The Focusing on Learner material makes an important distinction: assessment should be used “for” learning. The purpose is not to classify learners. It is to gather information that helps learning move forward. [short pause] This mindset changes how a teacher uses evidence. A response that shows misunderstanding is not merely a mark against the learner; it is information about what needs clarification or further practice. A strong response is also useful because it reveals readiness to continue or deepen the work. Assessment for learning keeps evidence connected to support, feedback, and the next teaching decision.

## Scene 009

[natural pace] [practical] [conversational]

ROOTS connects assessment with probing. Probing is proactive: the teacher creates opportunities to keep track of learning so that decisions or feedback can follow. Assessing is reactive: the teacher uses available opportunities and learner responses to understand outcomes and the overall experience. [short pause] Together, they help the teacher remain attentive during learning. A purposeful question can reveal current understanding. Observation during practice can reveal how a skill is developing. The important point is to use evidence deliberately, not to wait until the end before discovering what learners needed.

## Scene 010

[natural pace] [clear] [teacher-focused]

Evidence can come from different strategies, depending on what needs to be understood. The ROOTS material includes different types of questioning, one-to-one conversation about learner work, assignments, observation during activities, written insight or reflection, summaries, answers, and demonstrations of skill. [short pause] No single strategy is automatically suitable for every outcome. A teacher chooses evidence that can reveal the intended learning and pays attention to what the evidence actually shows. This prevents assessment from becoming a routine task disconnected from the decision the teacher needs to make.

## Scene 011

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [steady]

Feedback follows naturally from evidence. When assessment reveals where learning stands, feedback can help the learner understand what is working and what should improve. The teacher may clarify an idea, ask a further question, identify a specific strength, or direct attention to the next part of a skill. [short pause] Feedback is most useful when it remains connected to the outcome and the evidence. General approval does not explain what has been achieved, and correction without guidance may not support the next attempt. ROOTS keeps feedback within a continuing process of learning, practice, and improvement.

## Scene 012

[natural pace] [inviting] [reflective]

Now select one learning outcome from a lesson you teach. Identify whether it mainly concerns knowledge, skill, or attitude. Then ask: what would learners need to say, produce, choose, explain, or demonstrate for me to see progress toward this outcome? [short pause] Compare that evidence with the content and activity you currently use. Do they prepare learners for the same result? This short review can reveal strong alignment or an important gap. The purpose is not to collect more assessment. It is to collect the right evidence and use it to support the next learning decision.

## Scene 013

[slow pace] [warm] [confident]

Learning outcomes describe what learners should know, do, or be willing to demonstrate. They guide the selection of content, activity, practice, and evidence. Assessment then helps teachers and learners understand what has been achieved and what support may be needed next. [short pause] In ROOTS, outcomes and assessment remain connected to the learner-centred experience: we teach toward clear learning and use evidence for learning and improvement. In the next lesson, we will connect these foundations with the FIRST Framework and see how its five domains and fifteen principles support ADLX as one coherent whole.
