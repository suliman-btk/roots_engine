## Scene 001

[natural pace] [warm] [welcoming]

السلام عليكم. Welcome back to ROOTS. In the previous lesson, we examined the learner-centred shift and the teacher decisions that keep attention on learning. At NAMA Foundation, we now turn to a central idea in ROOTS: Active Deep Learner eXperience, or ADLX. [short pause] By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain the three connected parts of ADLX and distinguish purposeful active learning from activity alone. As we begin, think of a time when learners appeared busy. What evidence showed that their activity also involved thought, meaning, and learning in your classroom?

## Scene 002

[natural pace] [clear] [conversational]

Let us begin with the learner experience. The ROOTS knowledge material describes it as any interaction, course, programme, or other experience in which learning takes place. It may happen in a school or classroom, outside school, outdoors, or through a digital environment. [short pause] A learner experience therefore includes more than one explanation or task. It includes the conditions, interactions, questions, activities, practice, reflection, and follow-up that shape what the learner encounters. ADLX asks us to consider how these parts work together as a complete experience.

## Scene 003

[natural pace] [engaged] [clear]

The first quality is active learning. In the ROOTS material, active learning involves learners in doing things and thinking about what they are doing. Learners do more than listen. They may read, write, discuss, solve problems, answer questions, formulate questions, explain ideas, or demonstrate a skill. [short pause] The specific action can vary, but the principle remains: learners participate in the work of learning. Their participation gives them opportunities to process ideas, practise, respond, and make their developing understanding visible to themselves and to the teacher.

## Scene 004

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [emphasis]

The words “and thinking” are essential. Visible activity does not automatically create active learning. Learners may complete steps without understanding their purpose, or finish a task without connecting it to the intended outcome. [short pause] In ROOTS, we ask what thinking the activity invites. Are learners making a decision, explaining a relationship, solving a problem, examining an idea, or reflecting on an experience? Purposeful activity connects learner action with mental engagement. This is why a lively classroom and an active learning experience are not always the same thing.

## Scene 005

[natural pace] [practical] [teacher-focused]

The teacher supports active learning by choosing participation that serves the outcome. If learners need to explain an idea, the experience should provide a meaningful opportunity to explain. If they need to demonstrate a skill, they need practice that makes the skill visible. If they need to solve a problem, they need a problem that requires the intended thinking. [short pause] The teacher also observes what happens during participation. Learner responses provide evidence that can guide feedback, clarification, further questioning, or another opportunity to practise. Activity and assessment can therefore support one another.

## Scene 006

[natural pace] [reflective] [steady]

The second quality is deep learning. The ROOTS material explains that deep learning arises from the need to understand and seek meaning. Learners relate concepts and ideas to existing experience and examine emerging knowledge for patterns and meaning. [short pause] Deep learning is therefore different from meeting information only at the surface. The learner works with an idea, connects it, questions it, and considers what it means. For a teacher, the important question becomes not only, “Did learners receive this information?” but also, “How did they make sense of it?”

## Scene 007

[natural pace] [warm] [explanatory]

Previous experience has an important place in deep learning. Learners bring knowledge and experience that can help them understand something new. When they relate a new idea to what they already know, they can compare, distinguish, extend, or reconsider their understanding. [short pause] The teacher can support this process by inviting learners to recall relevant experience, identify connections, and explain how the new learning relates to what they have encountered before. The purpose is not simply to mention an experience. It is to use that connection to develop meaning and support further learning.

## Scene 008

[natural pace] [analytical] [clear]

Deep learning also involves analysis and evaluation. Learners examine new knowledge rather than accepting or repeating it without thought. They look for relationships, patterns, reasons, and meaning. They consider how ideas connect and what those connections reveal. [short pause] This does not require every lesson to become an abstract debate. It requires the level of thinking to match the intended learning. A carefully chosen question, comparison, explanation, reflection, or application can help learners move beyond recall and engage more deeply with the knowledge or skill they are developing.

## Scene 009

[natural pace] [confident] [engaged]

ADLX brings the active and deep qualities together within the complete learner experience. Active learning creates meaningful participation. Deep learning develops connection, analysis, and meaning. The learner experience brings attention to how the entire journey is designed and facilitated. [short pause] These parts strengthen one another. Activity can provide experience for reflection. Reflection can reveal meaning in activity. Previous knowledge can guide participation, while practice can test and deepen understanding. In ROOTS, ADLX is formed when these qualities work together rather than appearing as isolated moments.

## Scene 010

[natural pace] [reassuring] [teacher-focused]

An enjoyable learner experience is not the same as entertainment without learning. Enjoyment may support participation, but ROOTS keeps the learning outcome in view. An impactful experience also requires more than a strong emotional moment. Learners need opportunities to think, connect, practise, review, and carry learning toward performance. [short pause] The teacher therefore considers both engagement and meaning. We ask whether learners were interested, but we also ask what they understood, what they could demonstrate, and how the experience helped them move toward the intended outcome.

## Scene 011

[natural pace] [practical] [steady]

Designing for ADLX requires attention before, during, and after an activity. Before it begins, learners need readiness and a clear connection with the learning purpose. During the activity, they need meaningful participation and appropriate support. Afterward, active review helps them examine the experience and identify what was learned. [short pause] Practice, feedback, sequence, and connection with reality also influence the depth of the experience. These considerations prepare us for the FIRST Framework, which organizes the principles that support the design and facilitation of ADLX.

## Scene 012

[natural pace] [inviting] [reflective]

Return to the lesson or activity you considered at the beginning. Ask four questions. What were learners doing? What were they thinking about while they did it? How did they connect the learning with previous knowledge or experience? What evidence showed meaning, understanding, or developing performance? [short pause] If one of these questions is difficult to answer, that identifies an area to examine—not a reason to add activity automatically. The next step is to adjust the purpose, question, practice, connection, or review so that the experience supports both active and deep learning.

## Scene 013

[slow pace] [warm] [confident]

Active Deep Learner eXperience combines meaningful participation, thoughtful connection, and the complete conditions through which learning develops. Active learning involves learners in doing and thinking. Deep learning helps them seek meaning, relate new ideas to experience, and examine knowledge carefully. [short pause] ADLX brings these qualities into one coherent learner experience directed toward learning and performance. In the next lesson, we will focus on learning outcomes and assessment: how to state what learners should demonstrate and how to use evidence to understand what has been achieved.
