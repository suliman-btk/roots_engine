## Scene 001

[natural pace] [warm] [welcoming]

السلام عليكم. Welcome to ROOTS, NAMA Foundation’s course for strengthening the learner experience through teacher practice. Let us begin with a question that matters in every classroom: how do you know that learning has truly happened? Finishing an explanation is not the same as seeing learning. Completing planned content does not automatically tell us what learners understood, practised, or can now apply. [short pause] In ROOTS, we focus on what learners encounter, what they do, how they think, what evidence they show, and how learning can continue beyond one activity or lesson.

## Scene 002

[natural pace] [reflective] [conversational]

Think for a moment about a lesson you taught recently. You may remember the content you explained, the questions you asked, and the activities you prepared. Now shift your attention to the learners. What were they doing? What ideas were they connecting? What responses showed understanding, confusion, readiness, or progress? [short pause] At NAMA Foundation, this change of viewpoint is central to ROOTS. We move from concentrating mainly on what the teacher delivers to examining the complete experience through which learners participate, think, practise, reflect, and demonstrate learning.

## Scene 003

[natural pace] [clear] [encouraging]

In ROOTS, we understand that a learner experience is broader than a presentation or an activity. It includes the setting, interactions, explanations, questions, practice, reflection, feedback, and follow-up through which learning takes place. It may happen in a classroom, outdoors, or in a digital environment. [short pause] The important point is how the parts of the experience work together. We will help you examine those parts deliberately, so that decisions about content, participation, sequence, and review remain connected to what learners are expected to achieve.

## Scene 004

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [teacher-focused]

This brings us to the learner-centred shift that guides ROOTS. Learner-centred teaching considers learners’ needs, differences, ideas, experiences, participation, and expected outcomes. It does not mean that the teacher becomes unimportant or leaves learners without direction. [short pause] We see the teacher’s role as more purposeful. The teacher prepares and explains, but also observes, asks, listens, guides, provides opportunities for practice, and uses evidence to decide what should happen next. We therefore examine effective teaching through both the teacher’s decisions and the learner’s responses.

## Scene 005

[natural pace] [engaged] [clear]

In ROOTS, we place Active Deep Learner eXperience at the centre. We use the abbreviation ADLX. Active learning means that learners are involved in doing things and thinking about what they are doing. Deep learning means that they seek meaning, connect new ideas with previous knowledge or experience, and examine learning carefully. [short pause] We bring these qualities together within the whole learner experience. Our purpose is not to keep learners busy. It is to support an enjoyable and impactful experience in which participation, thought, meaning, and practice strengthen learning.

## Scene 006

[natural pace] [emphasis] [conversational]

Notice the difference between activity and purposeful activity. A learner may be moving, speaking, or completing a task, but we still need to ask: what thinking does this activity require, and how does it support the intended learning? [short pause] Through ROOTS, we connect active involvement with deeper meaning. Learners need opportunities to engage, connect ideas, review experience, and recognize what they have learned. This is why we do not present ADLX as one technique. We use it to consider the quality and coherence of the complete experience from the learner’s point of view.

## Scene 007

[natural pace] [clear] [confident]

To support ADLX within ROOTS, we use the FIRST Framework. FIRST contains five connected domains and fifteen principles, with three principles in each domain. F focuses on learner behaviour. I focuses on interaction within positive group dynamics. R focuses on reviewing activities through readiness, activity, and active review. S focuses on sequencing learning. T focuses on transforming learning into performance. [short pause] We do not treat these domains as separate classroom tricks. We connect them as layers that guide how a learning experience is designed and facilitated.

## Scene 008

[natural pace] [explanatory] [steady]

Here is how we use the framework in ROOTS. We begin by understanding learners and the evidence in their behaviour. We consider the conditions that shape interaction, motivation, and attention. We prepare learners, involve them in purposeful activity, and help them review the experience actively. We organize ideas and activities into a coherent sequence. Finally, we connect learning with reality, practice, continuity, and performance. [short pause] As we study each principle together, keep the whole picture in mind: FIRST supports the complete Active Deep Learner eXperience we seek to develop.

## Scene 009

[natural pace] [practical] [teacher-focused]

Learning outcomes keep our work in ROOTS focused. An outcome describes significant learning that learners can reliably demonstrate. It directs attention to what learners should know, be able to do, or be willing to demonstrate—not simply to what the teacher intends to cover. [short pause] Assessment then provides evidence of what has been achieved and what may require further support. We connect content, teacher decisions, learner activity, and evaluation with the intended outcomes. This alignment helps us judge learning through evidence instead of relying only on impressions or completed content.

## Scene 010

[natural pace] [supportive] [clear]

We also connect ROOTS directly with four areas of teacher practice. Learner eXperience Design, or LXD, concerns designing or redesigning learning through outcomes, ADLX, and FIRST. Learner eXperience Facilitation, or LXF, concerns guiding that experience in practice. Learner eXperience Coaching, or LXC, and Mentoring and Coaching, or MnC, support listening, questioning, feedback, feedforward, reflection, and improvement. [short pause] Within ROOTS, we address LXC and MnC at teacher application level. We do not present them as professional coaching certification.

## Scene 011

[natural pace] [reassuring] [reflective]

In ROOTS, we connect your progress with evidence and the course outcomes. Understanding will be checked through questions, situations, and reflection. Application will be shown by examining learning, designing or redesigning an experience, planning facilitation, considering a teacher-level coaching case, and reviewing evidence from practice. [short pause] We do not treat evaluation as a collection of disconnected tasks. Each piece of evidence should relate to content we have already taught and to an outcome you are expected to achieve. In this way, evaluation remains a meaningful part of the learning journey.

## Scene 012

[slow pace] [warm] [encouraging]

Let us close by returning to our opening question: how do you know that learning has truly happened? In ROOTS, we answer through the learner’s experience and through evidence. ADLX describes an active and deep experience in which learners participate, think, connect, practise, and make meaning. FIRST provides five connected domains and fifteen principles that help us design and facilitate that experience. [short pause] Before the next lesson, choose one lesson you know well and ask: what were my learners expected to experience, do, and demonstrate? Together, we will next explore learner-centred teaching and the teacher’s role.
