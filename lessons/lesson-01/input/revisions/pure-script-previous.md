## Scene 001

[natural pace] [warm] [confident]

السلام عليكم ورحمة الله وبركاته. Welcome to ROOTS, a professional learning course designed for teachers. Throughout this course, the main focus is the learner experience: what learners encounter, what they do, how they think, what they can demonstrate, and how learning continues after a lesson ends. [short pause] ROOTS will introduce Active Deep Learner eXperience, the FIRST Framework, and practical ways to design, facilitate, review, and improve learning. You will complete the journey individually through the LMS and connect each part of the course to your own teaching context.

## Scene 002

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

A learner experience can take place in a classroom, school, outdoor setting, digital environment, or another setting where learning occurs. It includes more than the information a teacher presents. It also includes the interactions, activities, questions, practice, reflection, and follow-up that shape how learners engage with learning. [short pause] This wider view is important because completing a presentation or finishing planned content does not, by itself, show what learners understood or can do. ROOTS therefore asks teachers to look carefully at both teaching actions and learner responses.

## Scene 003

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]

The course begins with a learner-centred shift. Learner-centred teaching focuses on learners’ needs, ideas, experiences, participation, and learning outcomes. It moves attention from what the teacher intends to cover toward what learners are expected to understand, demonstrate, practise, and continue applying. [short pause] This shift does not remove the teacher’s role. It makes the role more purposeful. The teacher still prepares content and guides the lesson, while also designing conditions in which learners can participate actively, connect ideas, receive feedback, and make meaning from their experience.

## Scene 004

[natural pace] [clear] [natural]

One central idea in ROOTS is Active Deep Learner eXperience, abbreviated as ADLX. Active learning involves learners in doing things and thinking about what they are doing. Deep learning involves seeking meaning, connecting new ideas with previous experience, and examining knowledge carefully. [short pause] ADLX brings active and deep learning together within a complete learner experience. The aim is not activity for its own sake. The aim is an enjoyable and impactful experience in which participation, thought, meaning, practice, and learning transfer support one another.

## Scene 005

[natural pace] [focus] [natural]

The FIRST Framework supports the creation of ADLX. The framework contains five domains and fifteen principles. Each domain contains three principles, and the domains work as connected layers rather than isolated techniques. [short pause] The five domains focus on learner behaviour, positive group dynamics, readiness and active review, the structure and sequence of learning, and the transformation of learning into performance. During the course, you will study every principle separately and then bring the full framework together when reviewing and designing a learning experience.

## Scene 006

[natural pace] [clear] [natural]

ROOTS contains seven units and twenty-five lessons. Unit 1 establishes the foundations: learner-centred teaching, the teacher’s role, ADLX, learning outcomes, assessment, and the connection between ADLX and FIRST. [short pause] Units 2 to 6 then examine the five FIRST domains and their fifteen principles. Each principle receives focused attention so that you can understand its meaning before combining it with the others. Unit 7 brings the course into teacher practice through design, facilitation, coaching, evaluation, and continued improvement.

## Scene 007

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

Later in the course, you will meet four related terms. Learner eXperience Design, or LXD, concerns learning design based on learner experience and the FIRST principles. Learner eXperience Facilitation, or LXF, concerns the teacher’s knowledge and skill in facilitating learning through the framework. Learner eXperience Coaching, or LXC, concerns coaching in an educational context using FIRST. Mentoring and Coaching, or MnC, supports reflection and improvement. [short pause] In this course, LXC and MnC are covered only at teacher application level, not as professional coaching certification.

## Scene 008

[natural pace] [engaged] [natural]

The course outcomes guide both the content and the evaluation. By the end, you should be able to explain ADLX and the learner-centred shift; define and connect the five FIRST domains and fifteen principles; design or redesign a learning experience through LXD; plan and demonstrate LXF; apply LXC and MnC at teacher level; and review evidence from your practice to prepare a continued-improvement plan. [short pause] Each unit contributes to these outcomes, and each evaluation task is connected to content that has already been taught.

## Scene 009

[natural pace] [clear] [natural]

Your evidence of learning will develop gradually. Short checks and classroom situations will help confirm your understanding. Individual reflection will help you connect the concepts to your own teaching. Later, you will prepare an LXD design artifact, plan or document an LXF application, work through a teacher-level coaching case, and bring your evidence together in a final portfolio and action plan. [short pause] The exact passing score, number of attempts, assessor role, certificate wording, and any recording requirement are not defined in this script and must be approved separately before LMS configuration.

## Scene 010

[natural pace] [reassuring] [natural]

Because this is a self-paced course, all required course activities are individual. You will not be required to form an online group or complete a mandatory paired task. Some lessons will discuss classroom interaction and positive group dynamics because these are part of the FIRST Framework. However, your own LMS work will use private reflection, knowledge checks, scenario decisions, design tasks, self-assessment, and classroom application. [short pause] This approach keeps the learning purpose of the original ROOTS journey while making the required course experience suitable for individual online study.

## Scene 011

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]

As you move through ROOTS, try to examine your teaching through evidence rather than assumption. Ask what learners were expected to experience and do. Notice how they responded. Consider what helped them participate, think, practise, and connect learning to reality. Then identify what you would retain or improve. [short pause] You are not expected to apply all fifteen principles at once. The course first helps you understand each principle, then connect the domains, and finally use the framework to examine and strengthen a learning experience in your own context.

## Scene 012

[slow pace] [confidence] [natural]

ROOTS begins with a clear direction: focus on the complete learner experience and on evidence of learning. ADLX describes the active and deep experience the course seeks to support. FIRST provides five connected domains and fifteen principles that guide design and facilitation. LXD, LXF, LXC, and MnC then help connect the framework with teacher practice and improvement. [short pause] Before continuing, record one thing you currently examine when deciding whether meaningful learning has occurred.
